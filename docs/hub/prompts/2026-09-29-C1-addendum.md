## The picture, in five facts

1. **Production is pearl-bound and the controller is inert.** Locally the host already sits near the field's unit
   curve; raising split value changes nothing; the one arm that raised pace (no pocket farming) lost win share. The
   live r100 deficit comes from what opponents do to our units, not from a production shortfall. **No task tunes
   production for its own sake.**
2. **The pace tax is death.** ~70 % of length lost by r150 is trapped deaths; a "salvage mill" in 1-wide 3-bed dead
   ends (Slithery (16,7), (23,7)) produced ~770 newborn deaths in one game. Economy mechanisms that eat more pearls
   (+50 % on Portals) turn them into length-2/3 dragons that die in crowding (Queen of Spades deaths 68 → 101) —
   −0.196 paired. Uneaten pearls at r100 are plentiful on open maps (Schooltime 63, Portals 64, Trauma 56): **supply
   is not the constraint; survival of what is produced is.**
3. **Control, not count.** What predicts winning is relative and spatial: units_share@100, territory@100,
   total_share@100 (AUC ≈ 0.80, F1); locally total r250 / longest r400 / h2h deaths (AUC 0.87–0.90, P1). Early
   concentration (top1_share@100) predicts losing. Every live loss reviewed says the same in words: late portal
   discovery → lost resource control; route convergence (too many dragons the same way); dead-end feeding with
   over-sized splits; long dragons killed by two-step dashes; a wall step where a split was available.
4. **Portals are half solved.** Atlas + probe/HOLD + own-body inference is **+0.11 paired** (28/15) on the fenrir
   host: portal steps 4× safer on Default and 3× on Schooltime, and the bot then uses portals 2–2.5× more (pearls
   343 → 470/game). On the Portals map itself (20 pairs) the per-step death rate did not move (0.2). A newborn whose
   rear passes a portal loses its neck from view and can step into itself: 61 of ~430 deaths on Portals seed 1;
   fixed by extending the body chain one cell across the portal.
5. **Python's boot turn is the cliff.** Imports + parse alone cost 46–51 M points on a process's first turn; the
   live control's own decision on top reaches 97.5 M and TLEs. The live control also throws two exceptions every
   game (`separation.py`: an undeclared global and a divide-by-zero on `bed_wait = 0`), dropping the dragon to a
   one-step fallback 8–35 times per 1k own turns.

## What changes per task

**C1-A (chassis, Opus 5.5).** Three additions, all cheap in C++:
- **Ship the atlas.** Embed the terrain of the ten public maps (edges, portal pairs, beds — build from
  `maps/*.map`; `tools/chaewon/build_atlas.py` and `tools/ouros2/build_atlas2.py` already do it for Python); on
  the first turn match the 7×7 view against the candidates of the map size and load the whole map. Every routing
  decision then starts from full knowledge on turn 1. Unknown maps fall back to the seen view.
- **Body chain across portals.** Reconstruct own body through portals (the neck is `dest(HEAD)[FACE+2]`; a newborn's
  segments keep the parent's facings). The chassis' one-step simulation must never allow a step into an own cell
  hidden behind a portal.
- **Measure the boot turn** under `--sandbox` separately from steady state and report it; the C++ chassis should be
  under 5 M on turn 1. This number alone is worth reporting to the lead the day it exists.
- The cheap opening policy stays cheap, but it must not enter a 1-wide dead end whose only exit is a split
  (the mill): dead ends are visible from the atlas.

**C1-B (routing, Opus 5.5).** The objective is **resource control, not pearl count**: value beds by who arrives first
(ours vs enemy arrival rounds from the same Dijkstra), value *coverage* (a dragon on a route no other own dragon
covers) so routes de-converge, reach portal exits early (the tyr review's most repeated loss), and treat dead ends
explicitly: enter only when the feeding value exceeds the minimum split cost to leave, and choose that split size.
Add to the ablation outcomes: territory@100 and units_share@100 from the F1 extractor
(`python -m tools.analysis.features extract …`), deaths by cause, and length lost by r150.

**C1-C (efficiency ledger, GLM 5.3).** The top leaks are already identified — trapped/mill deaths, crowding deaths of
L2/3 dragons, newborn deaths, portal exits — so the ledger's job is to *rank them by length lost* per map for us vs
band vs top, then fix in that order on the chassis. Use the F1 extractor for the per-death tables rather than writing
new code. The field itself churns (38.5 newborn deaths per 100 births, wall+self 10.8 per 1k dragon-turns): the
target is the band's number, not zero.

**C1-D (portals, GLM 5.3).** Start from `bots/ouroboros-s02-portal`'s measured baseline, not from nothing: port the
atlas + probe + HOLD + own-body inference rules to the chassis, then attack the unsolved case — the dense 20-pair
Portals map, where the per-step rate stays at 0.2: probe every adjacent pair before transit, price blind exits from
the observed rate, and the id-parity convention against double transit. The fall in deaths must not come from
fewer transits (the +0.11 came from *more* of them).

**C1-E (targets, GLM 5.3).** Two corrections. Targets are **per map, not per class**: u100 medians run from
Slithery 61.5 and Schooltime 26 down to Queen of Spades 9.5 and Dilemma 5.5, and a class target held the bot back
on Schooltime. And the numbers that matter are **relative and spatial**: compute units_share@100, territory@100,
total_share@100 (F1 extractor, unchanged on corpus replays) alongside the raw curve, plus the churn rates (newborn
deaths per 100 births, wall+self per 1k dragon-turns, length lost by r150) per cohort. P1's 300-game corpus cut is a
prior, not the answer; the corpus now holds ~5,000.
