## Why an open lane now (the lead's question)

Open exploration was the whole programme for its first week and it produced Tyr V12, the bot Ares is a port of. It
was then stopped in favour of directed tasks because three things were missing: a field-relative yardstick (bots were
only compared with each other, so "beats the zoo" was circular), CPU headroom (the winning ideas were search, and
Python had none), and parity tooling (rewrites lost behaviours silently). All three exist now — BENCHMARKS.md on a
28k-replay corpus, Ares in C++ with ~10× the search budget, the golden harness. That is exactly the point at which
chess engines stopped being directed: Stockfish's progress is thousands of one-patch tests through a fixed gate, not
hypotheses from a director. So the answer is yes: an open lane is now the cheapest source of gain, and the directed
tasks shrink to what an open lane will not do on its own (infrastructure, measurement, the tuner). The lane is
bounded by three rules that stop its known failure modes: the gate is field-relative, not zoo win rate (no self-play
overfit); one mechanism per version (attribution stays readable); and the generalisation panel is reported beside
the pool (no learning the ten maps).
