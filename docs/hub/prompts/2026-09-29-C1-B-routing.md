## 0. The question

Is the early game lost because our routing is crude, and does the C++ headroom turn into pearls by round 100?
The chassis leaves **~95 M of the 100 M points per dragon-turn** unused (whole turn 3.9 M; a whole-map BFS 0.43 M
on 32×32, 0.86 M on 60×40; each dragon is its own process with its own budget). Two deliverables: a router that
spends that budget on **resource control** — reaching beds and portal exits before the enemy, spreading own
dragons so they do not converge or enclose each other — and the three-arm ablation that answers the lead's
question ("is complex logic costing us the early game?").

What the ledger settled before you start (so you do not re-derive it): income and effort are *inside* the band's
range; the deficit is death-side — trapped/mill (Portals 126 vs band 63 length lost per 1k dragon-turns, Slithery
111 vs 88, Schooltime 25 vs 4), newborn deaths (Portals 81 vs 33), portal exits (28 deaths per 100 steps vs
Cutlery's 12.5), small-dragon crowding (Portals 50 vs 28). Pearls are not scarce on open maps (Schooltime 63 uneaten
at r100). The router's job is therefore **safe, de-conflicted arrival**, not raw speed.

## 1. The binding constraint: tournament maps are unseen

Everything in `…C1-out-of-sample-rule.md` applies. In this task specifically:

- **No map special cases.** The earlier draft said "special-case Prisoners Dilemma: hold and deny". Delete that
  idea in that form. If a behaviour is right on PD, it is right because PD is small, bed-poor and contact comes
  early — so key it on *measured* tile count, reachable bed count by r50 and first enemy-contact distance, and it
  must fire on any unseen map with that signature and stay off on the rest.
- **All router parameters key on structure the bot measures**: tile count (width × height, known from turn 1),
  kelp density in the area seen so far, portal pairs discovered, bed density and observed spawn gaps, own and
  visible enemy unit counts. Horizon, candidate caps and enemy-conservatism are functions of those; write the
  functions, not per-map tables.
- **Atlas off is the real bot.** Build and measure with `ATLAS_ENABLED` off *and* on. With it off, the router works
  from the seen area plus per-process memory (`World::mem`, portal pairing as discovered, `dives` for unpaired
  portals). Report both. If the router's edge exists only with the atlas on, it is not a router result.
- **The generalisation panel is part of every measurement**: `maps/var/*_tr.map`, `maps/pub/*_rec.map` (transforms
  of the live maps; the atlas cannot match them) and the 20-map synthetic suite in `maps/new/` (weights in
  `maps/new/training_map_weights.csv`; read `maps/new/EXPLAINER.md`). Report the live pool and the panel separately.

## 2. The router — `bots/cx-b01-router`

- **Time-aware per-dragon Dijkstra** from the head through portals with wrap and kelp edges, over cells that
  *vacate* over time: the chassis already gives each other-dragon cell a `vac` depth (first arrival depth at which
  it is free, from chain order plus hidden-tail credit) and `build_block_mask` for own segments (segment i free at
  depth i + 2). Enemy heads: block their sprint reach, `min(len − 1, 2)`, at depth 1 (the chassis rule) and their
  likely next cells after that. Output per dragon: arrival round at every cell. Budget: ≤ 2 M points per dragon on
  32×32, ≤ 5 M on 60×40. Cost it under `--sandbox` with `tools/cx/meter.py` (purge the wasm cache: the 1.2.2
  sandbox hashes only `.cpp`, so header edits silently reuse old builds — `tools/cx/arena.py` does this for you).
- **Bed valuation**: for each bed the round its pearl is ripe (countdown), the pearl on the ground now, and the
  *observed* spawn gap (`spawn_at` / `pearl_seen` in the world model); value = pearls we can eat there before the
  enemy's earliest arrival, over a horizon of ~40 rounds, discounted; include the next ripening as well as the
  current one, so a dragon that will arrive early **waits and patrols** rather than wandering (the Schooltime
  swarm: workers loop a ~5×5 patch around their bed cluster and eat each pearl the round it spawns).
- **Assignment with spacing**: one bed *cluster* per dragon, clusters at least ~3 cells from other allied heads
  (the top ten's structure: nearest allied head at median distance 2, one head per cluster). Greedy on
  value-minus-arrival with a pair-swap pass (Hungarian if it fits the budget — measure). No two own dragons routed
  to the same cell in the same round. Route de-convergence is an explicit term: penalise a path that shares cells
  with another own dragon's planned path in the next k rounds (the 28 Sep loss review: "too many dragons take the
  same route" on Devil and Default). The chassis' exact one-step simulation keeps the final say.
- **Enclosure-aware routing (leak #1)**: the reach-5 flood is nearly free in C++ — run it per candidate step and
  never plan into a region whose reachable cells are fewer than the room need (the chassis' `flood_room` /
  `room_need`); never enter a 1-wide dead end whose only exit is a split unless the feeding value exceeds the
  minimum split cost to leave, and then choose that split size (leak #8, the dead-end feeding rule from the Tyr
  review). Break an enclosure toward open space even at pearl cost.
- **Splits are routing decisions (leak #2)**: the split rule is pearl-gated, not round-gated — split the moment
  length reaches 4, into 2 + 2 (95 %+ of top-ten Schooltime splits), while the unit cap allows and while the
  child's first bed exists: choose the child's first target *at split time* from the arrival map (earliest
  crowd-free bed near the tail), and do not split when no such target is within the horizon. Newborn deaths within
  10 rounds are the statistic this must move (Portals: 60 of 100 newborns dead by r10 vs the band's 35).
- **Kelp in the cost map (leak #6)**: kelp-adjacent cells carry a cost when the room behind them is small
  (Slithery: our wall deaths 26 vs band 9 per 1k). Do not avoid kelp per se — the band eats faster on Slithery
  than the top ten and dies doing it; the top ten's wall deaths are 0.
- **Portals as routes, not risks**: transit volume is field-normal and the +0.11 portal-safety result came from
  transiting *more* with known exits. The router treats a portal with a known exit (paired, or exit memory) as an
  ordinary edge; unknown landings are C1-D's problem (probe, exit memory) — expose a hook (`exit_known(pair)`)
  and do not duplicate their rules.
- **Sprint discipline (leak #5)**: a sprint (k-step move costing k − 1 segments) only when the arrival margin at the
  target beats the enemy's and the pearl pays back within the horizon; cap sprints per dragon per k rounds. Our
  sprint tax is 0.023 length per pearl vs the band's 0.
- **Size-keyed frugality** (the lead's note): candidate caps for beds and for other own dragons in the assignment
  step scale with tile count and unit count; small maps run everything, large maps cap to the nearest k. A
  parameter, keyed on measured structure, so it is one line to tune.
- Every parameter (horizon, discount, enemy conservatism, spacing radius, split-near-bed radius, caps) in
  `params.hpp` with a comment and a default; add your block at the end.

## 3. The ablation (the lead's issue 1) — three arms on identical seeded fixtures

- (a) `bots/yuna-v03-core` as it is (Python, the current logic).
- (b) `bots/anna-a02-chassis` with its cheap opening policy (C++, no search).
- (c) `bots/cx-b01-router` (C++, the compute spent on routing).
- Fixtures: ten live maps × both sides × seeds 1–3 against the panel in the index (`bots/yuna-v03-core`,
  `bots/fenrir-v18-arrival-ready-beds`, `bots/kazuha-s01-swarm-dissolve`, plus `bots/ouroboros-s02-portal`), **and**
  the generalisation panel (`maps/var`, `maps/pub`, `maps/new`) — arm (c) both with the atlas on and off. Sandbox
  points recorded for every arm on the four probe fixtures. `tools/cx/bench.py` and `golden.py` exist; use them.
- Outcome variables, exact pairs (map, side, seed, opponent): units r25/r50/r100, total r100/r250, first-pearl round
  (team and per child), pearls eaten by r100, pearls per 100 dragon-turns, moves per pearl, deaths by cause per 1k
  dragon-turns (wall/self/ally-body/h2h/portal-step), length lost by r150, and territory@100 / units_share@100 from
  the F1 extractor (`python -m tools.analysis.features extract …`), and the game result. Report (b)−(a), (c)−(b),
  (c)−(a) with pairs better/same/worse and sign-test p, live pool and generalisation panel separately.
- Reading: (b) is already known to lose 60/60 to (a) at r100 pace (C1-A), so "complexity is costing us" is off the
  table; the question is (c). If (c) − (b) ≥ +4 units at r100 or ≥ +6 pearls by r100 with trapped and newborn
  losses not above (b)'s, the compute converts; if (c) beats (b) on the pool but not on the panel, it learned the
  pool; if neither, routing is not the lever and the director redirects. Say which.
