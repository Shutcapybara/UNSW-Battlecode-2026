# C1-B — route finding and bed assignment; the complexity ablation  (model: **Opus 5.5**)

Shared rules: `docs/hub/prompts/2026-09-29-C1-index.md`. Depends on C1-A's chassis (`bots/cx-a01-chassis`); until
it lands, read the chassis prompt, `bots/yuna-v03-core/world.py` and `bots/fry-v03-portal-hunters/hunter.cpp`, and
draft the algorithms against the helper's data structures. Bots `cx-b01-router`, `cx-b02-…`.

## 0. The question

Is the early game lost because our routing is crude (Python can afford ~4 map-wide computations a turn), and does the
C++ headroom (300) turn into pearls by round 100? Two deliverables: a router that spends the compute, and the
three-arm ablation that answers the lead's question ("is complex logic costing us the early game?").

## 1. The router — `bots/cx-b01-router`

- **Time-aware per-dragon Dijkstra** from the head through portals with wrap and kelp edges, with body cells of all
  dragons treated as obstacles that *vacate* over time (a body cell of a dragon of length L moving away frees after
  its tail passes: model as a per-cell "free from round" estimate from facing and length; be conservative for enemy
  bodies). Output: for each dragon, arrival round at every cell. Cost it: ≤ 2 M points per dragon on 32×32, ≤ 5 M on
  Schooltime, so 16 dragons fit under 60 M total with the parse.
- **Bed valuation**: for each bed, the round its pearl is ripe (`pearlIn`), the pearl on the ground now, and the
  arrival round of every own and enemy dragon; value = pearls we can eat there before an enemy can, over a horizon of
  ~40 rounds, discounted; include the *next* ripening as well as the current one, so a dragon that will arrive early
  waits on the bed rather than wandering (pre-positioning: hunter-v15 ate 3× the pearls of a reactive bot this way).
- **Assignment**: one bed per dragon, conflicts resolved by a greedy on value-minus-arrival with a second pass that
  swaps pairs (a Hungarian assignment is fine if it fits the budget; measure). No two own dragons routed to the same
  cell in the same round; the chassis' exact one-step simulation still has the final say on the step taken.
- **Splits are routing decisions**: a split from the rear creates a child at the tail; prefer splitting when the tail
  is near an unclaimed bed so the child has a target on birth (newborn deaths within 10 rounds are a tracked loss).
- Every parameter (horizon, discount, enemy conservatism, split-near-bed radius) in `params.hpp`.

## 2. The ablation (the lead's issue 1) — three arms on identical seeded fixtures

- (a) `bots/yuna-v03-core` as it is (Python, the current logic).
- (b) `bots/cx-a01-chassis` with the cheap opening policy (C++, no search).
- (c) `bots/cx-b01-router` (C++, the compute spent on routing).
- Fixtures: ten live maps × both sides × seeds 1–3 against the panel in the index; sandbox points recorded for every
  arm on the four probe fixtures. Outcome variables, exact pairs: units r25/r50/r100, total r100/r250, first-pearl
  round, pearls eaten by r100, deaths by cause per 1k dragon-turns, and the game result. Report (b)−(a), (c)−(b),
  (c)−(a) with pairs better/same/worse and sign-test p.
- Reading: if (b) ≥ (a) on pace, the Python logic was costing us; if (c) > (b) by ≥ +4 units at r100 or ≥ +6 pearls
  by r100, the compute converts; if neither, the pace gap is not routing and the director redirects. Say which.
