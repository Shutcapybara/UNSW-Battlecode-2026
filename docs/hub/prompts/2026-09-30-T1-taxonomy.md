# T-1 — The taxonomy: a structured map of the problem space and the opportunity space (Opus 5.5, long-lived session)

Lineage: as given by the lead. Branch `r/<lineage>`. Output: `docs/TAXONOMY.md` (one document, structured, kept current)
plus `docs/taxonomy/` for the per-entry detail it links to. Host: either machine; no games are run in this task.
Read-only over everything else: never edit other lanes' trees, findings or the ledger — propose ledger changes in your
status file (`claude/<lineage>-status.md`) and the director applies them.

## What this is, and is not

Not a temporal log (the decisions file and the findings are that). A **taxonomy**: a collection of the features the
bots compute, the algorithm designs they use, the problems that have been identified, and the solutions attempted
for each, cross-referenced, with what the evidence says. Its purpose is to let the lead conceptualise the problem
space and the opportunity space better than the current scatter of findings allows, and to let anyone ask "has
this been tried, on what, with what result, and why did it stop" in one lookup. You will be asked to update it
periodically for the rest of the project; design it to be updated, not rewritten.

## The structure (build it in this order; each is a section of `docs/TAXONOMY.md` with a table and links)

1. **State and features** — everything a bot knows or computes: per-cell memories (seen-age, pearl memory,
   bed timing, terrain, portals), per-dragon scalars (momentum, TTLs, mode), team-level state (sonar payloads,
   handoff packets), and the analysis-side features (the feature lab, benchmarks references, the ledgers). For
   each: which lineages have it, how it is computed, its CPU cost where known, what consumes it, and what
   evidence exists that it matters (a finding, an ablation, a tuner sensitivity, or nothing).
2. **Algorithm designs** — the decision architectures: bounded target search and its caps; the move scorer and
   its terms; split logic (routine, escape, cramped); portal handling; threat and support evaluation; sonar
   emission and receipt; the validity wrapper; imitation and learned components; the phase guards. For each: the
   lineages that use it, the parameters that shape it, what has been varied and what happened, and what has
   never been varied.
3. **Problems** — the named failure classes: trapped/enclosure deaths, newborn deaths, portal-exit deaths,
   crowding, churn (corpse-eating), opening-vs-late trade-offs, map-identity dependence, pool fit, seat asymmetry,
   convergence in fights, timeouts (historical), oscillation, and any you find in the code or findings that lack
   a name. For each: the measurement that defines it, its size against the field (the three-number form), which
   maps and phases it concentrates in, and its ledger row.
4. **Attempted solutions** — every mechanism tried against a problem, across all lineages (Python and C++,
   teammates' and models'): what it did, on which base, the result with the number that decided it, the verdict
   (accept / hold / reject / local hold / never measured), and *why it stopped* — the reason a line of work
   ended is the most useful field in the whole document and is usually missing from the findings.
5. **Cross-reference matrices** — problems × solutions (what has been thrown at what), solutions × lineages
   (who has what), features × consumers, and the map × mechanism table as it accumulates from M-1 and the lanes.
6. **The opportunity space** — derived from the above, not asserted: cells in the matrices that are empty
   (problems with no attempted solution; features computed but consumed by nothing; mechanisms measured on the
   wrong host only), lines that stopped for reasons that no longer hold (a CPU limit that C++ removed; a gate that
   D-032 replaced; a host that was the wrong lineage), and contradictions between findings that need a decisive
   test. Rank them by the size of the problem they address and the cost to test, and say which existing lane
   each belongs to.

## Conventions

Every entry has a stable id (`F-…`, `A-…`, `P-…`, `S-…` for the four kinds), a one-line definition, the lineages
it appears in, the evidence pointer (file and section, or bot path and line), and a status. Numbers are quoted
from findings with their source; if two findings disagree, record both and the discrepancy (there is at least one:
the Ares V06 and Lune scorecards differ in absolute level on identical games). Entries never say "good" or "bad";
they say what was measured. Keep `docs/TAXONOMY.md` navigable (tables, anchors, under ~400 lines) and push detail
to `docs/taxonomy/<id>.md`. Commit and push after each section. On every later "update" request: diff the
findings, decisions, ledger and lane status files since your last commit (`git log --since`), add or amend the
entries they touch, re-derive §6, and report what changed in the status file.
