# S-23 — Target claims and ownership

**Kind:** Solution · **Status:** hold · **Ledger:** L12, L33 (sciel-05 named as instance) · **Last reviewed:** 2026-09-30

Decide which ally owns a pearl or bed — by a broadcast claim, by closest visible teammate, by known route distance, or
by a central assignment — so that allies stop converging on the same target.

## Verdict and why it stopped
- Verdict: hold as a family. Accepted forms exist only on old Python hosts (hunter-v06, fry-v14); on Ares the only
  measurement is removing the host's discount (06a, rejected); the explicit claim protocol for Ares (sciel-05) was
  never built.
- Why it stopped: bifrost — "No tested iteration has shown a broad gain worth replacing V01"; router — "Routing is not
  the lever. Survival is"; Sciel — the named next step after the valuation-time guards failed ([S-22](S-22.md)) is
  arrival-level claims, left unbuilt at the end of the lane (inferred: lane stopped at 04b).
- Revival condition: the strongest live pointer in the programme — L12 (0.7) with Sciel's "the unlock must be
  arrival-level deconfliction (target claims)"; d1 lists "target claims / arrival-level deconfliction (Sciel-05
  proposal)" as a candidate ledger row; L33 names sciel-05 as a concrete instance of coupled beliefs over sonar.
  Host: every measured claim/ownership form was on Python or the chassis, not on the production line.
