## Readiness and disagreement with Antioch's targets

**None of these opening anchors is stable enough to replace gate references.** There are only 40 sampled games per
map and 5–11 top-ten sides per map. A 500-resample series/game-cluster bootstrap, refitting both the field ECDF and
top-ten median, gives a median r50 opening percentile interval width of **0.441**, maximum **0.888**. Full intervals
are in `uncertainty.csv`. They do not capture missing-team bias or stale ranks. A zero-width interval for an all-zero
metric means no variation in this sample, not a well-validated strategic target.

Proposed release criteria (a Himeji policy proposal, not an existing gate): at least 200 independent game/series
blocks per map, at least 50 top-ten sides spanning five teams per map, fresh ladder and at least eight of the current
top ten represented across the reference, cluster-bootstrap 95% percentile width <=0.10, and an independent later
window moving the anchor by <=0.05 percentile. Rare queen-survival events need their own precision criterion.
Keep the same frozen reference for candidate and parent. Refresh after Antioch's full post-era build and new data.

Antioch's section remains unchanged. Himeji disagrees with these readings:

1. A both-side complete-field round-limit win of **0.53** is impossible; the mean is 0.5 by construction. If that
   row refers to a restricted cohort, name the restriction and denominator. Our assertion checks pass on 211 games.
2. Antioch's **10.5** is survivor-only end length in its finding, but is labeled r490 in its target table. Preserve
   checkpoint and conditioning. Our sample gives unconditional r490 median 0, survivor-only median 10.
3. **Queen survival >=0.5** is a proposed intervention objective, not a measured top-ten percentile. It may be a
   useful aspiration on feasible long-game structures; label it as such and keep it separate from field references.
4. "Losses with a material lead" must specify conditioning. Here the top-ten figures 60% and 18.2% describe the same
   six games with different denominators. Both are published in `endgame.csv`.

**Tie to testers:** Carthage and Rome should report the same actual-reach r490 columns and both conditional loss
rates on their paired seeds, plus overall win. Their result will determine which operational objective is useful;
the field observations alone do not establish an intervention's causal gain.

### Live hypothesis H-H1 — preserve the queen's head piece without stopping all production

Proposed ledger link L24/L39 (new form; weight 0.5). When the queen splits in an escapable low-reach state, retaining
more length in the parent/head piece should preserve the queen with less opening cost than prohibiting productive
queen splits altogether. The parent keeps the queen ID; sending most material to the child does not save her.
This complements Carthage's guard/nosplit tests and does not claim to rescue a structurally sealed pocket.

Falsifier: the paired 95% upper bound on the r150 queen-survival gain is <=0, or the overall-win upper bound is <0,
or the presumed production advantage over a blanket nosplit arm does not appear in bed income/splits and r100
material. A zero-crossing interval is inconclusive, not disproof. Test a single allocation change, not a new bot
family. Suits Rome after its 1.2.3 baseline; Carthage's existing arms supply a useful comparison.

Size: screen on the fixed pool, then seeds 1–3 on both panels, paired by seed/map/opponent/seat. The old resolution
table's ~70/80 side-games for units/length half-SD is a planning guide, not queen power. For a paired binary outcome
with target delta .10, n ~ (1.96+.84)^2*(q-.01)/.01 = **149, 306, 463 pairs** for discordance q=.2,.4,.6.
Pilot-estimate q and inflate for repeated-fixture clustering; a blanket "300 games resolves ten points" is not
guaranteed. Smaller diluted overall effects need more games. Keep overall win and r100/r250 material as guards.
