# Renoir lane report 1 (R-2 lane `ra`)

**The short version.** I built the loop (`tools/ra/`) and a base, `renoir-00-base`. It is Ares V06 verbatim, with
golden parity of 0 divergent turns out of 16,715, and its scorecard matches the V06 finding to the third decimal.
I then screened 28 single mechanisms against the BENCHMARKS gate. **None passed.** There are three kinds of result.

1. **Moving an existing Tyr parameter away from its tuned value in the cautious direction always loses economy.**
   This covers room needed to enter a space, trap penalty, pocket farming, bed anticipation and child room: −0.01
   to −0.18 econ~.
2. **Moving the same parameters in the risk-taking direction helps a little, but far less than the gate needs.**
   Less threat aversion, less revisit penalty and a lighter trap penalty each come out at +0.01 to +0.02 econ~ on
   160 games. That is 3–5× short of +0.05, and usually comes with a small drop in units@100 or in win rate.
3. **Two new-code mechanisms made large changes, both negative.** Exploration value by information gain scored
   −0.22 and cluster-aware target value −0.03 with win −0.14. So the evaluation has levers that move the economy
   a lot; the ones found so far move it the wrong way.

One finding matters beyond the lane. **V06's pool edge contains map-shape special cases.** Its three `devil_*` terms
fire only when W==32 && H==16. On the transposed Devil map the base wins 17 %, against 100 % on Devil itself.

## What kinds of mechanism paid

- **None reached +0.05, and the size of the gap is the finding.** The best mechanisms, the risk-taking moves 17a/d,
  18a/c and 14b, sit at +0.012 to +0.023 on 160 games. The paired bootstrap interval on 160 games is about ±0.03, so
  a single seed resolves +0.05 only when the true effect is about +0.08. Seat-A screens overstate: the two that went
  to 160 lost half their effect (+0.044 -> +0.019, +0.033 -> +0.019).
- **Caution costs economy in this lineage.** Every change that cut self-inflicted deaths cost economy one for one
  or worse. Examples: 12a cut kelp deaths 20 % and lost 0.18 econ~; 03b cut them 15 % and lost 0.06. The trapped
  and mill deaths are the price of pocket farming and swarm density. Farming alone is worth 0.62 econ~ on Autarky.
  On Ares the C1-C leaks are the economy's own exhaust. A leak fix has to change *which* pockets are entered, not
  how many, or it will not pay.
- **Waiting loses and exploring is map-dependent.** Standing near a bed that is about to ripen (01a/c, 11) loses on
  dense maps more than it gains on sparse ones. More exploration (07b, 16) wins big on QoS (+0.3–0.5) and loses on
  Devil, Schooltime and Trophy. The same QoS/Trauma-up, Devil/Schooltime-down trade recurs across a third of the
  candidates.
- **Churn is in the metric.** 38 % of what the base eats is ally corpses, and it has 443 deaths per game, mostly
  length 2–3. `pearls@k` rewards churn, and the length@100 guard binds on almost every positive candidate.

## Generalisation finding: V06 has map-shape special cases

`policy.hpp` has three terms that fire only when `w.W == 32 && w.H == 16`:

- `devil_center_bonus`: pull toward the x midline until r42.
- `devil_lane_bonus`: y lanes by id until r100.
- `ally_body_buffer`: keep off ally bodies.

**Ablation (renoir-23, full pool):** turning the three terms off costs −0.042 econ~ and −0.075 win rate. All of
that comes from Devil (econ −1.22 at r50–r250, win 1.00 -> 0.31). On Prisoners Dilemma, the other 32x16 map in the
pool, the same terms **hurt**: econ +0.13 and win 0.62 -> 0.75 without them. They are Devil's opening, keyed on
Devil's size. On the pool Devil is won 16/16. On `var/devil_tr` (the same map transposed to 16x32) the base wins 17 %. Trophy
also drops, from 80 % to 17 % on `trophy_tr`. Overall gen win is 0.524 against a pool 0.762 over the same three
zoo opponents plus V06.

Keying on width and height is technically "measured structure". In effect it is map identity, and a 32x16 map with
different content would get Devil's opening. This part of V06's pool edge will not carry to unseen maps. A
structural replacement would be a midline race keyed on the spawn geometry the bot can observe. It cannot pass the
current gate (pool econ unchanged), so I have not built it. It belongs with the director as a Sprint/Qualifier
risk.

## Recommendations to the director

1. **The +0.05 single-seed bar is out of reach for one-mechanism patches on a tuned Tyr descendant.** Fishtest
   works because its bound matches patch size: it uses SPRT on ~0.5–2 Elo. Here, a lane gate like "paired
   seeds 1–3 (480 games), bootstrap lower bound of d econ~ > 0, units/length and win not down" would admit the
   +0.02 risk-taking patches and keep out noise. As written, the lane can only produce rejections.
2. If the bar stays, the lane should move from parameters to evaluation structure: *which* pocket, *which* bed,
   *when* to explore as a function of measured supply. Those are the levers with large effects (16: −0.22, 12a:
   −0.18). They need designs that keep the dense-map farming and add the sparse-map exploration, rather than trading
   one for the other.
3. Retire or generalise the `W==32 && H==16` terms before the Qualifier (10 Oct, unseen maps).

## Operational notes

- The Cowork VM shell kills background jobs, caps calls at 180 s and is shared with other sessions. Big maps take
  90–130 s there, which is why `lane.py --budget` starts heavy maps first and never late. The cloud container
  (2 cores) runs a background queue. Together they manage ~6–15 games/min, so about 3 screens per hour.
- `r/ra` was committed with a temporary index (plumbing), so `main`'s working tree was not touched. The hub-git
  sweeper had already committed the lane's bot directories to `main`.
- The VM cannot delete files, so `git add` left `tmp_obj_*` files under `.git/objects/*/`. They are harmless and
  `git prune` removes them.
