# C1-B — route finding, bed assignment and the complexity ablation (cx-b01 / cx-b02-router)

1. **What was built.**
   - `bots/cx-b02-router`, on `anna-a02-chassis`. The ablation's arm (c).
   - Its `CX_ATLAS 0` twin, `cx-b02-router-noatlas`.
   - The first cut, `cx-b01-router` (+ its noatlas twin), kept as a snapshot.
   - The ablation: 3 arms × 4 opponents on the live pool (seeds 1–3) and on the 31-map generalisation panel (seed 1), plus arm (c) with the atlas off on the live pool. 1,704 games.
   - `tools/cx/ablate.py`, and new per-game early-game statistics in `tools/cx/arena.py` and `bench.py`.
2. **The deciding numbers (c − b, exact pairs).**
   - Live pool: total length at r100 is better in **176 / same 3 / worse 61** pairs (p < 0.001; medians 36 vs 23). Pearls by r100: 100 vs 35 (194/1/45). Units at r100: **118/11/111 (p = 0.69)**.
   - Unseen panel (`var/` + `new/`, where the atlas cannot load): length r100 **126/12/94 (p = 0.036; 26 vs 24)**. Pearls **104/9/119 (n.s.)**. Units **101/21/110 (n.s.)**.
3. **Reading.**
   - The compute does convert into pearls and length on the live pool, but most of that is the atlas plus a mill on three maps (see §4).
   - On unseen maps the router gives a small length gain (+2) with fewer losses than (b), and no pearl or unit gain.
   - (c) does **not** meet the lead's bar. The live pool shows +4 units in medians but not in pairs, and +65 pearls. But newborn and trapped losses are far above (b)'s on the live pool, and the pearl edge vanishes on the panel.
   - **Routing is not the lever. Survival is** (as the C1-C ledger said).
4. **Sandbox (vs yuna-v03, probe fixtures).**
   - Points per turn: p50 4.4–6.6 M, p99 ≤ 11.7 M, max 16.2 M.
   - The boot turn rises to ≤ 12.6 M (the chassis was 4.9 M).
   - 0 faults.
   - Policy cost on top of the chassis is 0.9–3.0 M per dragon-turn (2 M budget on 32×32, 5 M on 60×40: met).
5. **What failed.**
   - Two falsifiers fired: (c) − (b) units r100 is not supported in pairs on the live pool (p = 0.69), and the live-pool pearl edge vanishes on the unseen panel and halves with the atlas off.
   - Wall and newborn deaths on the live pool are 3–9× (b)'s. The cause is the dead-end "mill" on Dilemma, Autarky and Slithery, plus swarm crowding on Schooltime.
   - The panel's `pub/*_rec` maps have edges identical to Portals and Slithery Fight, so the atlas matches them. They are not an unseen test, and I excluded them from the clean panel.
6. **Next.**
   - Stop investing in routing breadth.
   - Take the router's arrival and dead-end machinery into a survival-first bot: no feeding in 1-wide trees unless the exit split lands the child in the core; crowd-aware room checks that are not over-conservative (my attempt cost pace); and a pre-r100 split cadence tied to measured room rather than to pearls.
   - Ship with the atlas off, unless the dev pass shows the panel unharmed with it on.

## The router (what `router.hpp` does, per dragon, per turn)
1. **Terrain 2-core peel** (cached per step-table version). Cells peeled away are dead-end trees, each with a depth and a tree id.
2. **Arrival maps.**
   - Own time-aware BFS: the chassis mask, with own segment i free at depth i + 2 and others at `vac`. With unit steps this is the Dijkstra the prompt asks for.
   - Enemy multi-source BFS from visible heads and heads seen within 8 rounds.
   - One BFS per visible ally (the nearest 6).
3. **Bed value.**
   - Counts the pearls eaten before the enemy's earliest arrival, over a horizon of clamp(√NC, 20, 60), discounted by 0.95 per step.
   - The first ripening counts in full; later ones at 0.3.
   - Respawn gaps are observed in play (then the atlas class, then a default of 24).
   - An unseen bed holds a pearl at the share observed at first sight (prior 0.3).
   - A cluster share of 0.6 is added for beds within 2.
   - A dead-end tree is valued only if the pearls inside pay the exit split (≥ 3).
4. **Assignment.**
   - Greedy over (me + visible allies) × candidate beds, with 3-cell spacing between targets.
   - A pair swap for me, and 1.3× hysteresis on my previous cluster.
   - The movement target is the cluster's best single cell.
5. **Movement.**
   - The chassis tiers keep the final say.
   - Within a tier the score is: patrol timing (arrive when the pearl spawns; never stand on the bed early), de-convergence from allies' planned paths (next 6 steps, strongest at step 1), dead-end depth and enemy proximity.
   - A paired portal is an ordinary route step when its landing has at least 2 open sides and lies outside every tree.
6. **Splits.**
   - Pearl-gated: split at length 4 into 2 + 2, before r200, when the child (head = our tail) has a target it reaches before us and before any visible ally.
   - The starters split in the first 3 rounds.
   - A trapped dragon splits so the child keeps len − 2.
   - Inside a dead-end tree with nothing left to eat, it splits the same way.
7. **Parameters.** Everything is in `params.hpp` ("C1-B router" block), keyed on tile count and on what the bot observes in play. No map identity is used anywhere.

**What each fix found:**
- **The "mill".** A long dragon feeds in a 1-wide dead-end lane, is boxed in and splits. The big child is born at the tail, which sits in the mouth of the next lane, so it repeats. Length stays level, but every cycle is a death: 49 by r100 on Dilemma, 48 on Autarky, ~150 on Slithery.
- **Unseen-bed chase.** Atlas beds without a countdown were over-valued, so dragons walked a line of beds without eating (Trophy). The fix values them at the share of beds seen holding a pearl.
- **Target dithering between clusters.** Fixed with hysteresis.
- **Patrol value beating pearls on the ground.** Seen on Devil; fixed by down-weighting later ripenings.
- **Blind portal landings into occupied 1-cell pockets.** A minimum-exits rule fixes this. Requiring 3 exits instead of 2 starved Portals entirely, because its pearls sit in 2×2 boxes.
- **Crowding rules** (value penalty, split gate, bigger room need) reduced deaths but cost more pace than they saved. Now off: `crowd_w = 0`, `split_crowd_max = 99`, `crowd_room_k = 0`.

### Reading against the prompt
- **(c) − (b) ≥ +4 units r100 or ≥ +6 pearls r100, with trapped and newborn losses not above (b)'s.**
  - Live pool: +65 pearls (medians) and +4 units in medians but not in pairs. Newborn deaths 18 vs 2 and length lost 148 vs 43: the losses are far above (b)'s. **Not met.**
  - Panel: +1 pearl, +1 unit; losses at or below (b)'s. **Not met** on the pace side.
- **"(c) beats (b) on the pool but not on the panel → it learned the pool."** Pearls and units: yes, it learned the pool. The atlas carries two thirds of the live-pool length gain (atlas off: +3 length and +20 pearls). Length is the one outcome that holds on the unseen panel (+2, p = 0.036).
- **Verdict: routing is not the lever.** The router converts compute into pearls where the map is known and the geometry rewards feeding, and pays for it in churn. On unseen maps it is roughly the chassis, slightly longer, with fewer losses. The deficit against the yuna lineage on the panel (units 10 vs 21, length 26 vs 49) is production that survives, not routing.
