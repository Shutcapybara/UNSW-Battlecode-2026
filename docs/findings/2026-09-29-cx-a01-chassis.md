# C1-A — C++ chassis and golden-decision harness (anna-a01 / anna-a02)

1. **Built:** `bots/anna-a01-chassis`, the protocol-3 C++ chassis (official helper, world model, portal-aware BFS API, map atlas, cheap opening policy); `bots/anna-a02-chassis` (a01 + split-when-trapped); `tools/cx/` (arena, bench, meter, golden, view, build_atlas). The bots use the "Anna" name the user asked for; they are C1-A's `cx-a01`.
2. **Deciding number:** under the sandbox the whole turn costs **p50 3.7–3.9 M, max 4.86 M** points on all four probe fixtures. The boot turn is **≤ 4.86 M** (target < 5 M). A 32×32 flood fill costs **0.43 M**. That leaves about 95 M of the 100 M budget for C1-B.
3. **Pairs:** a02 vs a01, 60 fixtures (10 maps × 2 sides × seeds 1–3, opponent yuna-v03): total length r100 better **33** / same 23 / worse 4 (sign test p < 0.001); units r100 30/25/5; pearls by r100 27/28/5. Against yuna itself both chassis bots lose 59–60 of 60 games.
4. **Sandbox:** p99 4.2–4.6 M, max 4.9 M. Zero faults or timeouts in 4 sandbox and 180 native games. Zero illegal actions (`no_action` deaths) in 60 + 60 games.
5. **Failed:** "zero wall/self deaths" is not met. a01 has wall 4.65 and self 0.31 deaths per 1k dragon-turns (yuna in the same games: about 10 and 5). a02 rises to wall 11.7 because a boxed-in dragon now spends its last turns splitting off 2-segment children, which then die. Portals is weak: most of its pearls sit in 2×2 boxes you can only enter through a portal whose landing is out of view, and the chassis treats those landings as blind (C1-D).
6. **Next:** C1-B/C/D build on `anna-a02-chassis` (same API as a01). Two open items belong to them: portal boxes (C1-D) and head-to-head losses to trading opponents, which run at 6–8 per 1k dragon-turns.

## API (what C1-B/C/D call)
- `anna::World` (`world.hpp`), rebuilt from the helper every turn in O(49 tiles + changed cells):
  - `dest(c,d)` is the engine step through portals and wrap. It returns BLOCKED, UNKNOWN or UNPAIRED (< 0) when there is no known landing. `step_opt` is the optimistic planning step.
  - Terrain: `ek`, `epid`, `pends` (portal pairs).
  - Pearls and beds: `bed`, `spawn_at`, `pearl_seen`, `seen`.
  - Other dragons: `parts` (each with `vac` = the first arrival depth at which the cell is free, from chain order plus hidden-tail credit), `enemy_heads`, `ally_heads`, `mem` (per other dragon: last head cell, facing, first and last round seen, visible length).
  - Own body: `body`, `own[]`, `body_offset`, `inferred`; also `last_exit_portal` and `born`.
  - `atlas`: index of the matched public map (-1 = none) and `atlas_bed` (bed class per cell).
- `nav.hpp`:
  - `dist(w, from, mask, grid, cap, max_depth, stop_at, n_stop)` is the BFS. It fills `grid.d`, `first`, `parent`, `order`, and `dives` (unpaired portals met).
  - `path(w, grid, to)`, `rev_dist(...)`, and `build_block_mask` (own segment i free at depth i + 2, others at `vac`).
- `policy.hpp`: `Policy::decide`. `flood_room` (time-aware, known terrain only, with unknown and unpaired-portal credits and tail-chase escape), `room_need` and `mark_danger` can be reused on their own.
- `params.hpp` holds every tunable with a comment each. Add your own block at the end.

## Deaths by cause (per 1k dragon-turns, whole game)
| | wall | self | body | h2h | illegal |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| a01 (60 games) | 4.65 | 0.31 | 0.80 | 6.57 | 0 |
| a02 (60 games) | 11.66 | 0.19 | 2.37 | 7.80 | 0 |
| yuna-v03 in the a01 games | ≈10 | ≈5 | ≈3 | ≈4.5 | — |

- **How the wall and self deaths happened:** I traced deaths with `tools/cx/view.py` and the `-DANNA_DEBUG` decision log. Almost none are a wrong step into kelp. They are dragons whose planned room was closed off, mostly by allies or enemies moving in, or dragons that walked into a 1-wide dead end before its end came into view.
- **Fixes made along the way:**
  - The room flood now walks known terrain only; an unknown edge credits 4 cells, not infinity. With the atlas, the terrain is fully known from turn 1.
  - The room need is max(len + 2, 2·len, 10).
  - A tail-chase escape counts only if the space it reaches is at least as big as the body.
  - Cells next to any other head are blocked at depth 1.
  - Chains that end at a portal or the view rim are given hidden segments.
  - Enemy reach uses its sprint range, min(len − 1, 2).
  - A split requires room for both halves.
- **Head-to-head deaths:** mostly yuna trading into our 2–3 segment dragons. The danger tier and the enemy-proximity penalty (−6 per step of closeness within 3) cut them, but only by about a third.

## Map atlas, portal body chain and dead ends
- **Atlas:** `tools/cx/build_atlas.py` writes `atlas.hpp`, 36 KB, all ten live maps, generated with the chaewon/ouroboros `mapview.load_map`.
  - `World::atlas_try` loads a map on the first turn of every process, including split children, if every edge kind and portal id in the 7×7 view agrees and exactly one map matches.
  - It matched on 100% of turns in the three maps I checked (Schooltime, Portals, Trophy).
  - It only covers maps as the files lay them out. A mirrored or transposed live layout would simply not match (safe fallback), not load wrongly.
- **Body chain across portals:** when the trail cannot be trusted (newborns, desync), the chain is walked from the head through the visible segments.
  - A hidden neck is taken as `dest(head, back)`.
  - A hidden predecessor of the rear segment is taken only if it is the rear's single out-of-view neighbour.
  - Segments that still cannot be placed shift the vacancy indices (`body_offset`), so a tail-side cell is never treated as freeing up early.
  - The one-step simulation marks as illegal any step into a known or inferred own cell. A landing out of view is at best tier DIVE.
- **1-wide dead ends:** a dead end fails the room need, so the policy only enters one when every alternative is worse. Before the atlas, an unseen corridor was treated as unbounded room; that was the first-hour bug (Portals start corridor x = 15).

## Golden harness (`tools/cx/golden.py`, usage in `tools/cx/README.md`)
- **Record** runs the game through unswbc's own engine and bot pools. The bot is launched exactly as `unswbc run` launches it and is neither changed nor copied. It stores every dragon's init, input blocks and replies as JSONL, optionally gzipped.
  - This deviates from the prompt's launcher wrapper under `bots/_golden/`. Recording at the engine callback captures the same bytes with no extra process and nothing added to `bots/`.
- **Replay** starts one fresh process per recorded dragon (compiled by unswbc from `bot.toml`, or `python3 main.py` with PYTHONHASHSEED=0). It diffs canonical replies: LOG, INDICATOR, DOT and LINE dropped; `--free-sonar` or `--ignore-sonar` for sonar payloads. It exits 1 on the first divergence, or after all of them with `--all`.
- **Checks:**
  - a01 on portals-B-1: 0 of 1,500 turns divergent.
  - a02 on default-A-1: 0 of 4,376 turns divergent.
  - yuna-v03 replayed against its own trauma recording: 0 divergent (Python is deterministic).
  - The chassis replayed against yuna's recording diverges on turn 0 (it sends no sonar), as it should.

## What the helper lacked
Nothing the protocol needs: echoes, portal ids on edges, pearl countdowns and PROTOCOL 3 are all in `helper.hpp`. Everything above that is in the World layer, not in a helper patch:
- portal pairing and exits;
- memory across turns;
- body order;
- other dragons' chain order.
