# C1-F — leak fixes on the chassis: what the baseline says, and what each fix moved

**Six lines.** What was built: the §1 baseline table (chassis atlas-on/off + yuna-v03-core,
live pool and the 31-map generalisation panel, 240/744 games per arm), then four
`params.hpp`-switched fixes on `bots/anna-a02-chassis` (via `bots/cx-f00-base`,
golden-identical to the chassis with the atlas on) — F-1 trapped/mill escape, F-2 portal
exit discipline, F-3 sprint discipline, F-4 kelp cost — each in its own directory, each
measured alone in exact pairs (F-2 in five configurations, F-3 in two), plus
`cx-f05-combined` on a four-cell live/panel × atlas on/off matrix. The number that
decides: **no fix passes its acceptance bar on the chassis — the ledger's leaks live in
the yuna-lineage code, not the chassis** (F-1 neutral 37/167/36 p=1.0; F-2's every
configuration either the sakura trap — steps −33%, deaths −30%, per-100-steps flat
25.9→27.1, pearls −4.4% — or bit-identical to base; F-3 negative — sprint/pearl 0.456
and 0.352 against the bar of ≤0.015, len pairs p=0.000/p=0.001; F-4 never flips a
decision, 2/235/3). Pairs: 240 exact pairs per arm on the live pool (plus 744-per-cell
panel matrix for f05); all against the same fixed 4-opponent panel, both sides, seeds
1–3. Sandbox: every bot p50 3.9 M, p99 ≤ 4.8 M, max 5.1 M, zero faults — the gate held
everywhere and the router's headroom is intact. What failed: gating the trapped split by
room size (it is Slithery's survival pump — 356 trapped vs 27 greedy splits per 100
rounds; a 10-cell requirement took u25 from 25 to 3 and eliminated the team), every
transit gate at chassis volume (the only active mechanism is the memory-fresh window, a
pure volume throttle; same-pair doubles need live-swarm volumes to exist — 40→39 across
arms), and sprinting in any disciplined form (a contested pearl pays 1 segment for a
denial that does not materialise). What next: f05's switch set (F-1+F-4 on, F-2 gates off
with `exit_known(pair)` + pair memory + sonar-probe plumbing live, F-3 governor off)
lands under C1-B's router as `params.hpp`; the atlas finding — +17% pearls, doubled
portal deaths, inert off-pool — is the router's portal-pricing brief. No CANDIDATE.toml
is filed; nothing is promoted.

The atlas buys economy and pays in portal exposure: with it, landings are remembered
(not "blind"), so the chassis transits more and dies more at exits. **The atlas is an
economy switch, not a safety switch — F-2's exit memory is what makes remembered
landings safe.** Off-pool the atlas cannot match (mirrored/transposed layouts), so the
panel cells below carry both settings.

## F-1 trapped/mill escape — `bots/cx-f01-escape`

Mechanism: per-turn enclosure probe (time-aware reach flood from the head; enclosed =
reach < min(room_need, len+2) with no tail-chase cycle); enclosed dragons steer to the
deepest reachable cell (the exit through vacating bodies) at pearl cost, room weighted
×16; the boxed-in split fires only when the child has ≥1 legal step (the a02 corpse
case); a 1-wide dead end whose only exit is a split stays out via the CRAMP tier (the
chassis's existing room check).

- **Pairs (240, live, atlas on): len_r100 37/167/36 (p=1.0); eaten_r100 36/177/27
  (p=0.31); deaths r100, wall r100, lenlost_r150 all flat.** Variant `f01b-roomneed`
  (fire at the full room_need bar) coincides with the chassis bit-for-bit on the smoke
  fixtures; not promoted.
- Statistic (trapped length lost per 1k on Portals/Slithery/Schooltime): not moved by
  arena-visible proxies; the chassis's trapped leak is small — **the ledger's trapped
  numbers measured yuna/live, and the chassis already contains the room-check tiers
  that prevent the mill.** Falsifier "a leak moved, not closed" did not fire — nothing
  moved.
- Two measured lessons that C1-B must keep: (1) `split_has_room` at len 2 has reversed
  iterator ranges (libc++ `length_error("vector")` — swallowed by the runner's
  catch-all; the fallback walker then drives the bot silently); (2) **never gate the
  trapped split by room size** — it is Slithery's survival pump (356 trapped vs 27
  greedy splits per 100 rounds; gating by `split_room_min` took u25 from 25 to 3 and
  eliminated the team at r28 on other configs).

**Verdict: neutral on the chassis; machinery (enclosure probe, exit target, corpse
gate) ships as switches for the router, where arrival-time traps reappear.**

## F-2 portal exit discipline — `bots/cx-f02-portals` and variants b/c/d/e — REJECTED on the chassis; infrastructure delivered

Mechanism (each its own switch): pair exit memory (survived transit = exit verified
clear for 8 rounds; seen-blocked bans the pair 6), one sonar ray per turn through the
adjacent blind portal (echo = counts by kind the next turn; transit on clean echo
only), id parity per pair edge, id-order exit simulation (no transit when a lower-id
head is adjacent to the landing), `exit_known(pair)` exposed for C1-B. Discipline
yields only when every alternative is certain death. Five configurations measured,
240 exact pairs each (live pool, atlas on):

| config | gates | steps | deaths | per-100-steps | pearls r100 | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| base | — | 3100 | 804 | 25.9 | 15075 | — |
| f02 (all rules) | memory+probe+parity+id-sim | 2151 (−31%) | 562 (−30%) | 26.1 | 14416 (−4.4%) | sakura trap |
| f02b confident | memory+probe+id-sim, fresh-exit penalty waiver | 2076 (−33%) | 563 (−30%) | 27.1 | 14416 (−4.4%) | sakura trap |
| f02c swarmwise | b, id-sim vs enemies only | 2076 (−33%) | 563 (−30%) | 27.1 | 14416 (−4.4%) | ≡ b to the digit |
| f02d memory only | memory gate + waiver only | 2076 (−33%) | 563 (−30%) | 27.1 | 14416 (−4.4%) | ≡ b to the digit |
| f02e confidence only | no gates, waiver only | 3100 (100%) | 804 (100%) | 25.9 | 15075 (0.0%) | bit-identical to base |

The b≡c≡d≡(totals) identity is the diagnosis: **on the chassis, probe-dirtiness,
parity and id-simulation never decide a transit — the only active mechanism in every
variant is the memory gate ("transit only within 8 rounds of a verified-clear exit"),
which is a pure volume throttle.** Per-map, Portals and Queen of Spades hold volume
and improve slightly (14.1→13.1, 33.6→32.8) because their transits repeat through few
pairs that stay memory-fresh; Default (210→52 steps), Schooltime (567→292) and
Slithery (617→401) spread across many pairs whose memory keeps expiring. And the
per-step rate never improves — the chassis's portal deaths are contact and
post-transit navigation deaths, which a crossing gate cannot see.

Falsifiers, both fired: "F-2 cutting deaths only by cutting transits" (fired on a/b/
c/d); "a fix moving its own statistic without the economy curve holding" (pearls
−4.4% on every gated variant). **Acceptance (−25% deaths, ≤10% transit loss, economy
flat) is not met by any configuration; the fix is rejected as a chassis patch.**

What survives for C1-B — and why this was still the right build: the World now carries
`pair_mem` (survived-exit memory, refreshed from view) and `exit_known(pair)`; the
sonar probe plumbing (one ray/turn, echo attribution) is in `main.cpp`/`Decision` and
measured free (sandbox p99 ≤ 4.8 M with it on); every gate is a `params.hpp` switch.
The router will transit at field volume (49/game, not 6) through remembered exits, and
THAT is the regime where C1-D's live evidence (28.2 vs 12.5 deaths/100 steps; 548
same-pair doubles) says the gates pay. The residual untested hypothesis, recorded for
C1-B: id-simulation at distance 2 with enemy sprint reach, and probe echoes that
ignore ally counts (dirty-only-on-enemy), against the router's real volumes.

Replay-level cross-check (`tools/replay_stats/portal_deaths.py`, the C1-D instrument,
run on saved fixture replays — 4 portal-heavy maps × both sides × seeds 1–3 × {yuna,
ouroboros-s02-portal}, 48 games per arm; every move command simulated through the
replay's own edge map, 889k walks verified with 0 mismatches):

| arm | steps/game (median) | near-portal deaths/game | per-100-steps | wall | self | h2h | same-pair doubles |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| base | 8.0 | 2.0 (153 total) | 20.5 | 21 | 0 | 61 | 40 |
| f02d | 2.0 | 1.0 (112 total) | 21.2 | 19 | 0 | 45 | 39 |

The exact instrument agrees with arena to the decimal: volume −75%, rate flat
(20.5→21.2), and **the same-pair doubles the parity convention targets are unchanged
(40→39)** — at chassis transit volumes, two allies are almost never in the same pair
inside three rounds; the doubles leak needs the live swarm's volume to exist. 40% of
near-portal deaths are h2h (contact at exits), which no crossing gate prevents.

## F-3 sprint discipline — `bots/cx-f03-sprint`, `bots/cx-f03b-closerace` — NEGATIVE, switch ships OFF

Mechanism: exact engine simulation of a MOVE path (ouroboros `tactics.sim` semantics —
the tail vacates per step, each step after the first cuts one segment, pearls eaten
mid-path keep their cell); sprint toward a pearl/bed target only when it wins a
contested arrival race; smallest sufficient k; rate-capped; no danger-marked cell on
the path; room check at the landing. The chassis never sprints (sprint/pearl = 0); the
live submissions grew it to 0.029–0.044 — this fix was to build the governor the
router needs.

Two configurations measured, both **negative** on 240/156 exact pairs (live, atlas on):

| config | trigger | cap | sprint/pearl | pearls r100 | len_r100 pairs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| f03 (first) | any enemy within equal terrain distance, k ≤ 3 | 4/24 rds | **0.456** (10–15× the live reference) | −0.4% (65/98/77, p=0.36) | 50/84/**106** (p=0.000) |
| f03b (close race) | enemy walks in no later than us, gap ≤ 1; k = 2, strictly first | 2/24 rds | **0.358** | +0.1% (flat) | 34/54/**68** (p=0.001) |

Reading: even the tightest disciplined sprint pays one segment per contested pearl and
the contested pearls are not actually denied (pearls flat) — on the chassis the race
model (terrain BFS from the target) fires constantly in contact and the denial almost
never materialises. The falsifier "sprint/pearl halves" fires against the chassis's own
0: any positive tax is a regression, and the length pairs confirm it. **The governor
mechanism is delivered and rate-controllable; the switch ships default-OFF.** The live
sprint tax (0.029–0.044) came from economisers sprinting *uncontested*; the chassis
already does the right thing by never sprinting, and C1-B's router should inherit
`f3_sprint = false` until it has a real arrival-margin model.

## F-4 kelp cost on tight ground — `bots/cx-f04-kelp` — folded into F-1 (the flood already covers it)

Mechanism: a cramped step (room flood below need) pays `f4_penalty` (6) per kelp edge
around the landing. Statistic: wall deaths/1k on Slithery (chassis 29.5 live-pool
median vs band 8.9 live).

**240 exact pairs: len_r100 2/235/3 — the surcharge never flips a decision.** The
chassis's room tie-break (`score += room_n` among cramped steps) already steers cramped
steps to the roomiest cell, which on kelp ground is the cell with the fewest kelp
edges; the two rules select the same step everywhere. **Folded into F-1 per the
prompt: the flood covers the tight-ground selection.** The Slithery wall rate (29.5/1k)
is a *navigation* leak (compact kelp corridors), not a *scoring* leak — the fix belongs
to C1-B's router cost map, as C1-C's ledger already suspected.

## What this task actually found (the director's read)

1. **The ledger's leaks are in the yuna-lineage code, not the chassis.** The chassis's
   own leak profile is different: wall deaths on Slithery (29.5/1k vs band 8.9 — a
   *navigation* leak that belongs to the router's cost map), portal deaths at
   25.9/100 steps but on one-eighth the field's transit volume, and an economy at
   0.4–0.7× the field median. C1-C's fix ranking was measured on live/yuna bots; the
   chassis needs the router before the death-side leaks reappear at volume.
2. **The atlas buys economy and pays in portal exposure** (pearls +17% p=0.08;
   portal deaths 72/143/25 p=0.000) and matches nothing off-pool (683/744 identical
   to atlas-off). Remembered landings make transits tempting, not safe — the
   router's portal pricing must consume F-2's `exit_known` memory, not the atlas.
3. **Two chassis mechanisms are load-bearing and must not be "fixed"**: the trapped
   split (Slithery's survival pump) and never-sprinting (the correct sprint policy
   until a real arrival-margin model exists — even minimal contested sprints lose
   length at flat pearls).
4. The falsifiers fired where they should: F-2's "deaths cut only by cutting
   transits" fired on four configurations; F-3's "sprint/pearl halves" fired against
   the chassis's zero. Negative results with exact pairs are the deliverable here —
   C1-B starts from a measured map of what doesn't work on this chassis.
